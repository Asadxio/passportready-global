    Good to Know

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Clear expectations are part of a better application experience.

    Can PassportReady guarantee my passport application will be approved?

    No. Under international and constitutional law, passport issuance is an exercise of sovereign state authority. Only the relevant government department (e.g. MEA in India, State Department in the US, HMPO in the UK) can determine legal eligibility and grant a travel document. PassportReady helps organize your documents and flag potential mechanical errors before submission.

    Does every country accept the same documents?

    No. Required documents, accepted alternatives, photo dimensions, fees, and procedures vary widely by issuing country, applicant age, and individual circumstance. Never assume a document accepted in one country is recognized in another.

    What should I do if my name is spelled differently on two documents?

    Do not assume that a mismatch is harmless or that an application agent can bypass it. Compare the records to determine which document is your legally recognized primary citizenship record. For minor typographical differences, two secondary IDs or an explanatory affidavit are typically required. For major name changes, requirements depend on jurisdiction: In India, routine marriage-related surname changes require only a self-declaration (Annexure 'E') without Gazette publication or newspaper notices; Gazette notification and two newspaper advertisements are required exclusively for major complete identity changes. The United States requires a certified court order or marriage certificate under 22 CFR § 51.25; the UK requires a deed poll; and Germany/France require official civil status registry amendments.

    Why is parental consent required for children under 16/18?

    Statutory frameworks worldwide enforce strict safeguards against international parental child abduction (e.g. US 22 CFR § 51.28, Indian MEA Annexure D/C, UK parental responsibility rules, and Schengen civil codes). Both legal parents or guardians must typically grant consent in person or via notarized forms, unless sole legal custody, death of a parent, or termination of parental rights is proven with certified court records.

    What is the international 6-month passport validity rule?

    Entry validity requirements are established by your destination country, not a single global rule. Many nations (such as Thailand, Singapore, and Indonesia) require at least 6 months of passport validity upon entry. The Schengen Area requires 3 months of validity beyond the intended date of departure (EU Regulation 2016/399), and passport issuance within the past 10 years. Under bilateral agreements (such as the US "Six-Month Club"), certain visitors only require validity for the duration of stay. Airlines enforce destination-specific rules via IATA Timatic.
