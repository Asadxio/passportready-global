    Good to Know

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Clear expectations are part of a better application experience.

    Can PassportReady guarantee my passport application will be approved?

    No. Passport issuance is an exclusive statutory authority exercised by sovereign governments under domestic legislation (such as the Indian Passports Act 1967, United States Code 8 U.S.C. § 1185 and 22 CFR Part 51, and the UK Royal Prerogative administered by HM Passport Office). Only appointed government passport and consular officers possess the legal authority to adjudicate citizenship, verify identity records, and issue travel documents. PassportReady provides self-audit checklists and pre-submission organizational guidance only.

    Does every country accept the same documents?

    No. Required documents, accepted alternatives, photo dimensions, fees, and procedures vary widely by issuing country, applicant age, and individual circumstance. Never assume a document accepted in one country is recognized in another.

    What should I do if my name is spelled differently on two documents?

    Do not assume that a mismatch is harmless or that an application agent can bypass it. Compare the records to determine which document is your legally recognized primary citizenship record. For minor typographical differences, two secondary IDs or an explanatory affidavit are typically required. For major name changes, requirements depend on jurisdiction: In India, a surname change after marriage needs the marriage certificate or a Joint Photo Declaration signed by both spouses (Annexure J), per the Passport Seva document advisor; a complete change of name follows the standard name-change procedure described on Passport Seva. The United States requires a certified court order or marriage certificate under 22 CFR § 51.25; the UK requires a deed poll; and Germany/France require official civil status registry amendments.

    Why is parental consent required for children under 16/18?

    Statutory frameworks worldwide enforce strict safeguards against international parental child abduction (e.g. US 22 CFR § 51.28, Indian MEA Annexure D/C, UK parental responsibility rules, and Schengen civil codes). Both legal parents or guardians must typically grant consent in person or via notarized forms, unless sole legal custody, death of a parent, or termination of parental rights is proven with certified court records.

    What is the international 6-month passport validity rule?

    Entry validity requirements are established by your destination country, not a single global rule. Many nations (such as Thailand, Singapore, and Indonesia) require at least 6 months of passport validity upon entry. The Schengen Area requires 3 months of validity beyond the intended date of departure (EU Regulation 2016/399), and passport issuance within the past 10 years. Under bilateral agreements (such as the US "Six-Month Club"), certain visitors only require validity for the duration of stay. Airlines enforce destination-specific rules via IATA Timatic.

    Does PassportReady store or share my personal information?

    No. PassportReady operates under strict privacy-by-design principles: zero collection of passport numbers, zero cloud storage of identity records, zero trackers, and 100% client-side browser evaluation.
