Research Methodology & Source Governance

How PassportReady researches, verifies, audits, and maintains passport statutory regulations, fee schedules, document alternatives, and biometric photo criteria.

The Golden Rule: Official Sources Only

Passport requirements are statutory and administrative rules enforced by sovereign authorities. Inaccurate advice can result in rejected applications, stranded travelers, or lost non-refundable government fees. Therefore, PassportReady follows a strict rule: We never guess, invent facts, or rely on secondary travel blogs.

Source Hierarchy Standards

Tier 1: Sovereign Primary Authorities (Mandatory for Rule Packs)

Every rule pack must be substantiated by direct documentation from the competent sovereign authority:

  • National Foreign Ministries & Departments of State (e.g. US Department of State, Ministry of External Affairs India, UK Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office).
  • Statutory Passport & Immigration Directorates (e.g. HM Passport Office UK, Jabatan Imigresen Malaysia, Dirección General de Pasaportes).
  • Official Government Gazettes & Administrative Codes (e.g. US Code of Federal Regulations Title 22, Passports Act 1967 (India), Malaysian Passport Act 1966).
Tier 2: Recognized International Standards Bodies

For cross-border interoperability and biometric specifications:

  • ICAO Doc 9303: Machine Readable Travel Documents (MRTD) published by the International Civil Aviation Organization, governing facial biometric geometry, MRZ line formats, and chip security.
  • ISO/IEC 19794-5: Biometric data interchange formats for photographic facial images.
  • Multilateral Border Agreements: Official texts such as Regulation (EU) 2016/399 (Schengen Borders Code).
Tier 3: Prohibited as Sources of Truth

The following are strictly banned from substantiating legal claims or fees on PassportReady:

  • Commercial visa expediting companies or third-party visa agencies.
  • Travel blogs, forums, Reddit threads, and unverified social media posts.
  • AI-generated text or syntheses without underlying government URLs and verification dates.

Country Status Taxonomy

Every jurisdiction in our directory is classified under one of three transparent statuses:

Verified Rule Pack Verified: Complete statutory coverage including fee schedules, accepted proof of identity/address/citizenship alternatives, photo specifications, age categories, processing pathways, and verified official portal URLs. All claims store a retrieved date and URL.

Portal Linked Portal Linked: The primary government passport authority portal, appointment system, and contact page have been officially validated and monitored, but detailed multi-concession rule packs are actively being compiled.

Awaiting Research Awaiting Research: Country indexed with ISO alpha-2 and alpha-3 codes, displaying generic baseline advisory questions with an explicit notice that local concessions and fee structures are awaiting official verification.

Audit Cycles & Stale Rule Protection

Government fees and passport rules change frequently. To prevent outdated advice:

  • 180-Day Review Window: Every verified rule pack stores a last_verified date. If a country's rule pack exceeds 180 days without manual re-audit, our UI automatically displays a prominent amber "Stale Rules Warning" advising users to double-check directly on the linked portal.
  • Automated Link & Hash Monitoring: An automated validator continuously tests official portal URLs to detect HTTP 404s, domain redirects, or significant content shifts.
  • Community & User Error Reports: Users, immigration attorneys, and consular personnel can report statutory updates directly via our Report Fee / Rule Update form.

Discrepancy & Name Resolution Logic

Our Name Discrepancy Analyzer classifies mismatches based on official consular guidelines (e.g. MEA Gazette notification rules, UK deed poll standards, US 22 CFR § 51.25):

  • Minor Discrepancy: Transliteration variants (e.g. Mohammed / Mohd), punctuation/hyphen differences, single-letter typographical slips, or expandable initials where birth and identity dates align.
  • Significant Discrepancy: Complete surname changes, omitted legal patronymics, or differing dates of birth, which mandate certified Gazette notifications, registered deeds, or court orders.
